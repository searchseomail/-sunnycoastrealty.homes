Real estate investment in Dubai: Average budget and hidden costs?

NeonNomad
Topic Starter
Posted: 26.01.2025 00:31
I am currently looking into property investment in Dubai and trying to understand the realistic entry price for a decent apartment in a good neighborhood. Aside from the property price itself, what are the mandatory additional fees, such as DLD fees and agency commissions, that I should account for? I would appreciate any advice from those who have recently purchased or are currently navigating the market.

Replies (12)

FadingFrequency
Replied: 29.01.2025 04:31
For a decent apartment in a prime area like Dubai Marina or Downtown, expect to start around 1.2M to 1.5M AED. Don't forget the 4% DLD fee, 2% agency commission, plus about 5k AED for trustee fees.
MysticMaverick
Replied: 06.02.2025 06:55
You should definitely budget an extra 7-8% on top of the purchase price for all the transaction costs. It's not just the DLD; there are also NOC fees from the developer and initial service charges.
RogueRebel
Replied: 24.02.2025 09:53
I recently bought in Business Bay. Aside from the 4% DLD, remember that if you are taking a mortgage, the bank valuation fee and mortgage registration fees add up quickly. It's a different world from understanding California property tax rates, so make sure to get a clear breakdown.
TitaniumTornado
Replied: 01.03.2025 01:39
Entry prices are rising, but you can find good units in JVC for around 800k AED. Always keep a cash buffer of at least 10% of the property value for the 'hidden' costs like connection fees and agent commissions.
VelvetVortex
Replied: 19.04.2025 06:27
Always check the service charges before signing! Some buildings have high maintenance fees that can really eat into your ROI if you are planning to rent the unit out.
NebulaNavigator
Replied: 06.05.2025 12:16
I was curious about global markets before settling on Dubai, and it's definitely easier here than in some restricted regions, unlike why is it impossible for foreigners to buy property in Abkhazia?. The process is straightforward, just prepare your paperwork early.
AstroArtisan
Replied: 16.09.2025 11:33
Don't forget the DEWA (utility) connection fees and the initial insurance costs. These are small, but they add up when you are doing your final budget.
XenialXenon
Replied: 17.09.2025 05:04
Agency commission is usually 2% + 5% VAT on that commission. Make sure your agent is RERA registered to avoid any legal headaches later on.
InfiniteIcon
Replied: 19.11.2025 09:11
If you are buying off-plan, the fees are slightly different, but for ready properties, the 4% DLD is non-negotiable. Ensure you have the cash liquid because banks don't cover those fees in the mortgage.
EchoEnigma
Replied: 24.11.2025 07:23
Location is everything. If you are looking for long-term appreciation, focus on areas near the new metro expansions. Expect to pay a premium, but it's worth it for the rental yield.
EmberExplorer
Replied: 23.12.2025 06:20
I spent months looking before pulling the trigger. My advice is to hire a good conveyancer who can handle the transfer process so you don't miss any of the mandatory registration steps.
JadeJuggernaut
Replied: 06.01.2026 00:28
It's a great market, but keep your expectations realistic. A 'decent' apartment in a prime neighborhood will rarely be below 1M AED nowadays. Budget for the unexpected maintenance costs that always pop up during the first year of ownership.
