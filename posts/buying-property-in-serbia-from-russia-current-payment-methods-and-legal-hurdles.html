Buying property in Serbia from Russia: Current payment methods and legal hurdles

ShadowScribe
Topic Starter
Posted: 03.03.2025 15:40
I am currently looking to purchase an apartment in Belgrade, but I am facing significant difficulties due to the current banking sanctions. Could anyone share their recent experience on how to legally transfer funds from a Russian bank to a Serbian seller? Additionally, I would like to know if it is possible to complete the transaction remotely via a power of attorney or if I must be physically present for the title registration. Any advice on reliable local real estate agencies that handle international clients would be greatly appreciated.

RustRider
Replied: 16.03.2025 15:45
Transferring funds directly from a Russian bank to a Serbian account is nearly impossible due to SWIFT restrictions. Most people I know are using intermediary accounts in countries like Kazakhstan or Turkey. It is a complex process, so check out this guide on the best countries for Russians to purchase real estate in 2024 to see if your strategy aligns with current market realities.
LunarLurker
Replied: 22.04.2025 08:33
You can definitely complete the purchase remotely using a Power of Attorney (Punomoć), but it must be notarized and apostilled. Make sure you find a reputable lawyer in Belgrade to draft it specifically for the property purchase to avoid any legal loopholes.
PlasmaProwler
Replied: 24.04.2025 09:40
I bought an apartment in Vračar last year. I had to travel there to open a local bank account first, as Serbian banks are very hesitant to accept direct transfers from Russia without a personal relationship with the client.
RetroRogue
Replied: 11.06.2025 02:46
Regarding the legal hurdles, the biggest challenge isn't just the money, it's the 'reciprocity' rule. Ensure your lawyer checks if a Russian citizen can legally hold title to the specific type of property you are eyeing, as there are sometimes restrictions on land ownership.

Do not attempt to transfer funds without consulting a Serbian tax advisor. The Anti-Money Laundering checks in Serbia are strict, and you will need to provide a clear 'proof of origin' for your funds to the bank.
PixelPioneer
Replied: 13.09.2025 09:13
Physical presence is not strictly required for the final registration if you have a solid PoA, but I strongly suggest being there for the final walkthrough. You don't want to buy an apartment sight-unseen in Belgrade, as the quality varies wildly.
RetroRogue
Replied: 16.09.2025 08:20
Crypto is sometimes mentioned in forums, but please be very careful. It is not a legally recognized way to buy real estate in Serbia, and you could lose your entire investment if the transaction is flagged by the authorities.
