Real estate investment in Spain: Budgeting and hidden costs

PixelPioneer
Topic Starter
Posted: 06.01.2025 11:52
I am currently looking into purchasing a property in Spain and am trying to get a realistic estimate of the total costs involved. Beyond the initial listing price, what are the typical taxes, notary fees, and legal expenses I should account for in my budget? I am interested in hearing from recent buyers about their experiences and any unexpected expenses they encountered during the process. Any advice on how much extra capital I should set aside would be greatly appreciated.

Rule of thumb is to add at least 10-12% on top of the purchase price for taxes and fees. If you are looking for advice on buying property abroad: where to start?, definitely budget for a good lawyer who isn't affiliated with the seller.
KrypticKnight
Replied: 21.03.2025 09:02
In my experience, ITP (Property Transfer Tax) varies by region, usually between 6% and 10%. Don't forget the AJD tax if you are getting a mortgage, plus the notary and land registry fees which are government-mandated.
ShadowScribe
Replied: 19.05.2025 10:33
Don't overlook the 'plusvalía' tax. While technically the seller's responsibility, many try to pass it onto the buyer in the contract. Always have your legal representative check this clause.
RetroRogue
Replied: 14.07.2025 04:05
I was surprised by the cost of changing utility contracts and the initial connection fees. Also, if you're buying a community property, ensure you get a certificate confirming the seller is up to date on community fees.
EchoEnigma
Replied: 30.07.2025 12:08
You should definitely look into residency options if you are spending a significant amount. Check out these best countries to invest in real estate for residency to see if you qualify for a Golden Visa.
FadingFrequency
Replied: 10.08.2025 13:10
My advice: hire an independent surveyor. It cost me about 600 euros, but it saved me from buying a place with major structural damp issues that weren't visible at first glance.
PixelPioneer
Replied: 24.08.2025 02:24
Budget for the NIE application fees and the translation costs for your documents. Everything needs to be translated into Spanish by a sworn translator if you are a non-resident.
JadeJuggernaut
Replied: 01.12.2025 14:28
I found that bank commissions for international transfers were higher than expected. Shop around for a currency exchange provider to save on the spread compared to using a standard high-street bank.
CrimsonCryptic
Replied: 08.12.2025 05:07
Remember that if you are buying a new build, you pay 10% VAT (IVA) instead of the ITP transfer tax. The legal fees for new builds are often higher due to the complexity of the paperwork.
IronIconoclast
Replied: 30.12.2025 08:58
Unexpected expense for me was the 'gestoría' fees. They handle all the tax filings and registrations, and while you can do it yourself, it's a nightmare for foreigners. Pay the professional to save your sanity.
QuartzQuasar
Replied: 15.01.2026 05:17
Make sure your budget includes the non-resident property tax (IRNR) that you have to pay annually even if you don't rent out the property. It caught me off guard the first year.
QuartzQuasar

Don't get distracted by virtual games or fake advice. Focus on the local market data. Some people ask about the best real estate investments for GTA Online in 2024, but please keep your focus on real-world legal due diligence when dealing with Spanish property.
