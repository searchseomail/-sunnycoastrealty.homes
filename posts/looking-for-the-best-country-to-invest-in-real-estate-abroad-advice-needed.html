TitaniumTornado
Topic Starter
Posted: 19.02.2025 08:43
I am currently looking to invest in international real estate for both rental yield and potential long-term capital appreciation. I am considering options in Southern Europe, Southeast Asia, and the Balkans, but I am struggling to weigh the risks regarding tax laws and residency requirements. Does anyone have experience with purchasing property in these regions as a foreign investor? I would appreciate any advice on which countries currently offer the most stable legal framework and transparent market conditions.

Southeast Asia offers incredible rental yields, especially in places like Thailand or Vietnam, but the ownership laws for foreigners are very restrictive. You usually need to set up a holding company or look into long-term leaseholds.
WanderingWarlock
Replied: 29.04.2025 12:53
The Balkans are an emerging market with lower entry costs, but the legal frameworks aren't as transparent as in the EU. Make sure you hire a local lawyer who is completely independent of the seller or agent.

Portugal used to be the gold standard, but the recent changes to their visa programs have shifted the landscape. It's still stable, but the 'easy' path to residency has definitely become more complex.
CosmicCoder
Replied: 17.08.2025 11:44
I invested in Montenegro a few years ago. It’s beautiful and the growth potential is high, but the bureaucracy is intense. Be prepared for a lot of paperwork and potential delays during the closing process.
NebulaNavigator
Replied: 18.08.2025 12:36
Always factor in the 'hidden' costs like property transfer taxes, notary fees, and ongoing maintenance. In some Mediterranean countries, these can add 10-15% to your initial investment cost.
XenialXenon
Replied: 01.02.2026 00:46
Transparency is key. I'd recommend sticking to countries that rank high on the Ease of Doing Business index. It might mean lower yields, but you'll have much better protection for your capital in the long run.
