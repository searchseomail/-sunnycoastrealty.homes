Buying Property Abroad as a Non-Resident: Best Countries?

CyberCenturion
Topic Starter
Posted: 18.01.2025 03:08
I am looking to invest in international real estate but I do not hold citizenship in any of the countries I am considering. Could anyone recommend stable markets where foreigners can purchase property without too many legal hurdles or residency requirements? I am specifically interested in countries that offer clear property rights and a straightforward buying process for non-residents. Any personal experiences or advice on where to start would be greatly appreciated.

Portugal has been my go-to recommendation. The process is very transparent, and there are no specific residency requirements for purchasing residential property. Just ensure you hire a local lawyer to handle the deeds.

If you are looking at the US, it is actually quite friendly to foreign investors. You don't need a green card or visa to own property here, though taxes can be tricky. Check out these steps to buying a house in the US as a non-resident to get a clear picture of the requirements.

Spain is a great option. I bought an apartment in Valencia last year and the bureaucracy wasn't as bad as people say, provided you have an NIE number. The property rights are very strong there.

Consider Thailand if you are looking for value, but be careful with the leasehold vs. freehold rules. I found this guide to buying property in Thailand for foreigners extremely helpful when I was researching the condo market in Bangkok.

Greece is worth a look, especially with the Golden Visa program, though you can buy property even without going for the visa route. It's a very straightforward process as long as you have a tax number.

Panama is highly underrated. They have laws that specifically encourage foreign investment, and the dollar-based economy adds a layer of stability that is hard to beat in Latin America.

I would suggest looking into Germany, though the market is quite competitive. As a non-resident, you can buy property, but you should definitely work with a local notary who is legally required to remain neutral during the transaction.

Honestly, Mexico is a popular choice for many, but remember the 'restricted zone' rules near the coast. You'll need a bank trust (fideicomiso), which is a common and safe way for foreigners to hold title.
