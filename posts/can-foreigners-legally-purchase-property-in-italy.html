Can foreigners legally purchase property in Italy?

CrimsonCryptic
Topic Starter
Posted: 28.01.2025 14:13
I am a non-EU citizen interested in investing in a vacation home in Tuscany. I have heard there are specific reciprocity agreements that affect who is eligible to buy real estate in Italy. Could someone clarify if there are any major legal hurdles for foreigners or if the process is straightforward for international buyers? I would appreciate any advice on the necessary documentation required for the purchase.

Replies (11)

MysticMaverick
Replied: 17.03.2025 05:25
Yes, you can absolutely buy property in Italy as a non-EU citizen. The key is the 'Condition of Reciprocity,' which means your home country must allow Italian citizens to purchase property there. Most Western countries are covered, but it is worth checking the list provided by the Italian Ministry of Foreign Affairs.
WanderingWarlock
Replied: 25.04.2025 05:04
The process is quite straightforward if you have the right documentation. You will primarily need a 'Codice Fiscale' (Italian tax code), which you can obtain from the Italian consulate in your home country before you start your search. It is essential for any legal transaction.
QuartzQuasar
Replied: 06.06.2025 08:33
I bought a home in Tuscany three years ago as a non-EU citizen. It wasn't as complicated as I feared, but you definitely need a good 'notaio' (notary). In Italy, the notary is a public official who represents the law, not just one party, so they ensure the title is clean.

One major hurdle is the bureaucracy. Make sure you have your passport and tax code ready, and be prepared for the 'atto di compravendita' (deed of sale) to be signed in front of a notary. It's a formal process, but very secure.

You don't need a residency permit to buy a vacation home in Italy, which is a common misconception. You only need a visa if you plan to stay for more than 90 days at a time. The purchase itself is purely an investment transaction.

I highly recommend hiring a 'geometra' or a surveyor to check the property for any unauthorized modifications. Italy has very strict building codes, and you don't want to inherit someone else's zoning violations.
