Can foreigners legally purchase property in Georgia?

NeonNomad
Topic Starter
Posted: 24.04.2025 08:20
I am currently considering investing in real estate in Tbilisi and would like to clarify the legal requirements for non-residents. Is it true that foreigners can buy residential and commercial property without any special permits or citizenship? Are there any specific restrictions on purchasing agricultural land for international buyers? Any insights or personal experiences from those who have already navigated the process would be greatly appreciated.

Yes, it is true! Foreigners can buy residential and commercial property in Georgia without any citizenship or special permits. It is one of the most open real estate markets in the world. Just bring your passport to the Public Service Hall.

I bought an apartment in Tbilisi last year and the process was incredibly fast. You just need your passport and a local notary. However, before you jump in, you should check out this article on whether now is the right time to buy to make sure you are getting the best value.
SolarSurfer
Replied: 11.06.2025 03:00
You are correct about residential and commercial property, but agricultural land is a different story. Foreigners cannot directly own agricultural land unless it is inherited or they have a specific legal status. Most investors stick to urban apartments.
MysticMaverick
Replied: 28.06.2025 02:25
The legal system in Georgia is very foreigner-friendly. I didn't need a lawyer, just a translator at the Public Service Hall. It is much simpler than buying property in other countries, like buying property in Thailand where the laws are quite complex.
RetroRogue
Replied: 30.06.2025 00:13
Don't worry about special permits. You have the same rights as a Georgian citizen when it comes to buying a house or office space. Just make sure to get a 'Statement of Ownership' from the Public Registry to verify the seller's title.
PlasmaProwler
Replied: 19.07.2025 14:11
I have been investing in Tbilisi for three years. The only real headache is the agricultural land restriction. If you are looking at land outside the city, clarify its 'status' first because the government is very strict about reclassifying agricultural plots.
RogueRebel
Replied: 09.10.2025 12:41
Process is straightforward: Sign the contract, go to the Public Service Hall, pay the fee, and you get the registry extract in a few days. It is very transparent compared to many other developing markets.
BlazeBard
Replied: 12.11.2025 06:46
Make sure you hire an independent real estate agent or a lawyer to do a 'due diligence' check on the property. Even though the laws are simple, you want to ensure there are no hidden liens or utility debts attached to the title before you sign.
