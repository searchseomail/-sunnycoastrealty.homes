Looking to buy property abroad: Which European countries are currently best for investment?

WanderingWarlock
Topic Starter
Posted: 21.01.2025 01:37
I am planning to kupit nedvizhimost (buy real estate) in Europe within the next six months for both personal use and long-term rental income. My budget is around 250,000 to 300,000 euros, and I am looking for locations with stable markets and good growth potential. Which countries currently offer the most favorable conditions and residency perks for foreign buyers? I would love to hear recommendations or warnings from anyone who has recently gone through the purchasing process.

Replies (13)

TitaniumTornado
Replied: 27.01.2025 11:46
Spain remains one of the top choices in that price range, especially if you look just outside Madrid or Barcelona, or along the Costa del Sol. With 300k, you can easily find a nice 2-bedroom apartment with great rental demand. Plus, while the Golden Visa is changing, there are still other residency pathways available.
SleekScout
Replied: 14.04.2025 11:34
Greece is definitely worth considering. Their Golden Visa threshold is increasing in popular areas to 500k, but there are still regions where you can invest 250k and get permanent residency for the whole family. The tourist rental market is booming right now, particularly in Athens and the islands.
QuantumQuill
Replied: 01.05.2025 08:07
Be very careful with bureaucracy in Italy. While you can find amazing bargains for 250k in the south or even historic apartments in the north, the buying process can take months longer than expected. Always hire an independent local lawyer who doesn't work with the agency.
ApexAlchemist
Replied: 05.06.2025 13:16
Portugal used to be the go-to, but since they ended the real estate option for the Golden Visa, it's harder for residency unless you go the fund route. However, for pure rental yield and lifestyle, cities like Porto and Braga still offer incredible value within your budget.
CosmicCoder
Replied: 15.06.2025 15:51
Cyprus is an underrated option. With 300k, you can buy a modern apartment in Larnaca or Paphos and qualify for their permanent residency program quite smoothly. The rental yields are stable because of the high number of expats and digital nomads moving there.
FadingFrequency
Replied: 10.07.2025 09:49
If you want absolute stability, look at Germany, though 300k won't get you much in Munich or Frankfurt anymore. You'd need to target smaller growing cities like Leipzig or Dortmund where gross yields are still decent and the market is rock solid.
TitaniumTornado
Replied: 31.10.2025 00:12
Montenegro is outside the EU, but it's heavily targeted for future integration and growing fast. Your budget of 300k can buy a luxury seaside apartment in Kotor or Tivat with great Airbnb potential, though residency perks are more limited compared to EU nations.
PlasmaProwler
Replied: 02.11.2025 11:04
Watch out for hidden costs! Many buyers forget that notary fees, property taxes, agent commissions, and legal fees can add up to 10-15% on top of the purchase price. Make sure to keep at least 30k aside for these expenses.
QuantumQuill
Replied: 27.11.2025 02:49
France has a fantastic long-term rental market, but taxes are notoriously high, both on purchase and rental income. If you buy in smaller cities like Lyon, Marseille, or student hubs like Toulouse, you'll get steady year-round tenants rather than just holiday makers.
ByteBandit
Replied: 03.12.2025 14:10
Hungary, specifically Budapest, offers incredible value for 250k-300k. You can buy multiple smaller apartments instead of just one, which diversifies your rental risk. The tourism is massive, and property prices are still lower than Western Europe with good upside potential.
FadingFrequency
Replied: 25.12.2025 02:37
I bought an apartment in Valencia last year. The process was stressful due to getting a Spanish tax number (NIE) and opening a local bank account as a non-resident, but the rental demand is incredible. My net yield is sitting around 6% right now.
BlazeBard
Replied: 30.12.2025 14:01
If residency is your main goal, look closely at Malta. They have strict property value minimums, but a 300k rental or purchase combined with their residency program makes it a great hub for international business and lifestyle.
RogueRebel
Replied: 08.01.2026 02:27
Turkey is a wild card. You can easily get citizenship with a 400k investment, but with your 300k budget, you can get a stunning property in Antalya or Alanya. Just be extremely cautious with inflation and currency fluctuations if you rely on local lira income, though most expat deals are in Euros or USD.
