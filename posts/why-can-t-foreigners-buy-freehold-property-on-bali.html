Why can't foreigners buy freehold property on Bali?

DigitalDrifter
Topic Starter
Posted: 18.01.2025 02:59
I have been looking into real estate investment opportunities in Bali, but I keep hearing conflicting information about ownership laws. Many sources state that foreigners are strictly prohibited from owning land under a freehold title, known as Hak Milik. Could someone clarify the legal restrictions for expats and explain the difference between Hak Pakai and Leasehold? I want to understand the risks involved before committing to any long-term investment.

Replies (8)

ObsidianOracle
Replied: 04.03.2025 04:20
The restriction on Hak Milik (Freehold) is strictly for Indonesian citizens only. As an expat, you are essentially limited to Hak Pakai (Right to Use) or Leasehold. If you are looking for more accessible markets, you might want to check this guide on foreign property ownership in other countries.
EchoEnigma
Replied: 05.04.2025 06:02
Hak Pakai is a registered title that allows foreigners to hold property legally for a set period, usually 30 years with extensions. It is much safer than a simple lease, but you must have a valid residency permit (KITAS).
VortexVagabond
Replied: 15.05.2025 02:26
Many people get confused because they use 'Leasehold' and 'Hak Pakai' interchangeably. A lease is basically a private contract with a landowner, whereas Hak Pakai is a government-recognized land title. Always use a reputable notary.
SolarSurfer
Replied: 15.05.2025 12:07
Be very careful with 'nominee' arrangements where you put the land in an Indonesian friend's name. It is illegal and you have zero legal protection if the relationship sours. Stick to legal structures like PT PMA.
InfiniteIcon
Replied: 08.08.2025 02:48
If your goal is long-term security, you should look into the specific requirements for PT PMA ownership, which allows a foreign-owned company to hold Hak Guna Bangunan (HGB) titles. It is a more robust, albeit expensive, route.
WanderingWarlock
Replied: 02.10.2025 14:59
The landscape of international real estate is constantly changing. If you are just starting out, it is worth comparing Bali to other markets in the list of best countries for property investment to see where your capital might be safer.
FadingFrequency
Replied: 20.10.2025 03:06
Leasehold is essentially renting the land for a long period. The risk is entirely dependent on the quality of your contract. If the lease isn't notarized or the land has multiple owners, you could lose your entire investment.
DigitalDrifter
Replied: 07.01.2026 09:19
Don't rush into anything. Bali real estate is a minefield of 'off-the-plan' projects that never finish. Always conduct thorough due diligence on the land certificate at the local BPN office before handing over any money.
