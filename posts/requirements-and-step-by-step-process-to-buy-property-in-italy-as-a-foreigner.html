Requirements and step-by-step process to buy property in Italy as a foreigner

CosmicCoder
Topic Starter
Posted: 07.01.2025 08:25
Hello everyone! I am seriously considering buying an apartment in Italy, preferably somewhere near the coast or in Tuscany, but I am totally confused about the legal requirements. What documents, taxes, and permissions do I actually need as a non-resident to complete a purchase successfully? Are there any hidden pitfalls or restrictions I should be aware of before starting my search?

Buying property in Italy as a foreigner is totally doable, but you definitely need a Codice Fiscale (Italian tax code) first. You can get this from the Italian embassy in your home country or once you arrive in Italy.
CrimsonCryptic
Replied: 08.05.2025 08:31
Make sure you open an Italian bank account early on, as all payments related to the purchase must be traceable. Also, if you are timing your purchase, you might want to read up on Navigating the Housing Market: When's the Best Time to Buy? to get a sense of seasonality.
SleekScout
Replied: 19.05.2025 13:40
As a non-resident, your main legal requirement is checking the reciprocity condition (condizione di reciprocità). If you are from the US, Canada, or EU, you're usually fine, but always double-check with a local notaio (notary).
IronIconoclast
Replied: 12.07.2025 12:25
Don't forget about the extra costs! Taxes, notary fees, and real estate agent commissions can add another 10% to 15% on top of the purchase price, especially if you are buying a second home.
PixelPioneer
Replied: 16.08.2025 09:40
If you are looking at rural Tuscany, pay very close attention to building permits and cadastral registry records. Many old farmhouses have unpermitted extensions that can stall a sale for months.
InfiniteIcon
Replied: 24.09.2025 14:29
The notary in Italy actually acts as an impartial public official who represents both the buyer and the seller, ensuring the title is completely clear of mortgages and liens before you hand over any money.

Always hire an independent bilingual lawyer who represents *only* you, not just the notaio. It gives you incredible peace of mind when reviewing the preliminary contract (compromesso).
