What documents and requirements are needed to buy property in Thailand as a foreigner?

StaticSpecter
Topic Starter
Posted: 14.02.2025 09:48
Hello everyone! I am seriously considering purchasing a condo in Phuket and want to understand the exact legal requirements and step-by-step process. What documents do I need to prepare, and is it possible to own land or only freehold apartments? Any advice from people who have already been through this would be greatly appreciated.

Buying a condo in Phuket is quite straightforward for foreigners as long as you stay within the 49% foreign freehold quota of the building. You mainly need your passport, a valid tourist or non-immigrant visa, and official bank letters proving the funds came from overseas in foreign currency.

BlazeBard

To answer your question about land vs condos: foreigners generally cannot own land directly in their own name in Thailand. You can only own freehold condominiums outright, or lease land long-term, typically through a 30-year leasehold agreement.
ApexAlchemist
Replied: 03.09.2025 00:56
Make sure you get a Foreign Exchange Transaction (FET) form from your Thai bank when you wire the money from abroad. The Land Department will require this document to register the condo under your foreign name.

Replied: 05.09.2025 12:06
Phuket is a gorgeous place, but property taxes and maintenance fees can sneak up on you. Before locking anything down, I was actually looking at where it is currently profitable to invest in real estate globally to compare potential yields against other markets.
KrypticKnight
Replied: 08.10.2025 02:11
You don't need a work permit or even residency to buy a condo. Just bring your passport, make sure the developer can prove the foreign quota is available, and use a reliable Thai lawyer to review the sales and purchase agreement.
GhostGuardian
Replied: 12.10.2025 06:11
Another crucial document is the Tor.Thor.3 form issued by your bank showing the inward remittance of foreign currency. Without it, the Land Department will not transfer the title deed into a foreigner's name.
MysticMaverick
Replied: 02.01.2026 14:37
Be very careful if someone tries to sell you land through a Thai company setup. While it's a common loophole used by some, the Thai government has been cracking down on nominee shareholders heavily in recent years.
CosmicCoder
Replied: 27.01.2026 07:52
Overall, the process takes about 2 to 4 weeks once you choose the unit and transfer the funds. Just make sure your paperwork is airtight and always use an independent legal advisor instead of the developer's recommended lawyer.
