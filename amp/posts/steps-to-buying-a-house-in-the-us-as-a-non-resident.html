Buying in the US as a non-resident is definitely doable, but you'll need an ITIN (Individual Taxpayer Identification Number) first. Most lenders will require a 30-40% down payment for foreign nationals. If you are exploring global options, you might find it interesting to look at a guide to buying property in Thailand for foreigners to see how different the requirements can be compared to the US.

You should focus on finding a 'Foreign National Mortgage' program. Many big banks won't touch these, so look for smaller boutique mortgage brokers who specialize in international clients. They will walk you through the credit check, which is usually done via a specialized international credit report service.

Documentation is everything. You'll need at least two years of tax returns from your home country, proof of funds, and a letter of reference from your home bank. Keep everything translated and notarized to avoid delays during the underwriting phase.

The closing process involves a title company. As a non-resident, the title company will need to ensure you are compliant with FIRPTA (Foreign Investment in Real Property Tax Act). Make sure your agent is experienced with this, or you could end up with a huge tax withholding surprise.

You don't necessarily need a green card, but you do need a valid passport and visa. If you have any questions about the legal structure, always consult with a real estate attorney before signing any purchase agreement. They can explain the difference between holding property in your own name versus an LLC.

Be patient with the mortgage underwriting. Since you don't have a US credit history, the bank has to manually verify your financial stability, which can take twice as long as a standard loan. Ensure your 'proof of funds' is liquid and easily verifiable in USD.
